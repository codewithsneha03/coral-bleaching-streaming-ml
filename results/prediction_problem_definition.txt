# Prediction Problem Definition
# Coral Bleaching Nowcasting from Environmental Conditions

## What is a single prediction example?

One prediction example = one coral reef sampling event where:
- A field survey recorded bleaching status (severity or percent bleached)
- Environmental measurements (SST, heat stress, etc.) were also recorded
- The sampling location and date are known

## What does the target mean?

**Binary classification target: `bleached`**

| Value | Meaning | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bleaching was observed at this site during this survey | Severity_Code in {1,2,3} OR Percent_Bleached > 0 |
| 0 | No bleaching was observed | Severity_Code = 0 OR Percent_Bleached = 0 |

Records with unknown or ambiguous outcomes are excluded (not labeled as 0).

## Which predictors are available?

41 numeric features from three categories:

1. **Temperature & heat stress** (34 features):
   ClimSST, Temperature_Kelvin/Mean/Min/Max, SSTA and variants (anomaly,
   DHW, frequency), TSA and variants. These describe the thermal
   environment at the reef during the sampling period.

2. **Site characteristics** (5 features):
   Latitude_Degrees, Longitude_Degrees, Distance_to_Shore, Exposure,
   Turbidity, Cyclone_Frequency.

3. **Sampling conditions** (2 features):
   Depth_m, Depth_m_missing (indicator for imputed depth).

## Is prediction contemporaneous or anticipatory?

**Contemporaneous (nowcasting).**

Environmental features and bleaching observations come from the SAME
sampling event.  The model answers: "Given these environmental conditions
measured at a reef right now, is bleaching present?"

This is NOT advance forecasting.  We are not predicting bleaching weeks
or months ahead.  This distinction is critical for honest reporting.

A nowcasting model is still useful: it can estimate bleaching at
unvisited reefs where only satellite-derived environmental data is
available, or screen sites for priority field surveys.

## Chronological ordering

Observations are sorted by sampling date (1980-2016).  The online
learning model processes them sequentially.  This means:

- The model is always evaluated on data it has not yet seen.
- Performance reflects how well the model generalizes to future
  conditions, not just random held-out data.
- Concept drift (changing relationships over time) can be detected.

## Site overlap between training and test

Multiple observations can come from the same site across different years.
In a strict spatial cross-validation, we would hold out entire sites.
For the streaming experiment, we accept temporal hold-out (evaluate on
future time periods) as the primary strategy, which is the natural
regime for online learning.

## Class imbalance

- 85.5% positive (bleached) vs 14.5% negative (not bleached)
- Ratio: 5.9:1
- This is a real property of the data: surveys disproportionately
  record bleaching events.
- Evaluation must use precision, recall, and F1, not just accuracy.
- A naive "always predict bleached" model gets 85.5% accuracy but
  is useless.

## Bleaching rate by decade

- 1980s: 100% (only 44 observations -- too few to draw conclusions)
- 1990s: 94.9% (1,701 observations)
- 2000s: 83.1% (7,840 observations)
- 2010s: 86.5% (2,304 observations)

The rate varies over time, which may manifest as concept drift in the
streaming model.
